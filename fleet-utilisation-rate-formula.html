Utilisation rate

Fleet utilisation rate: formula and calculator

Also spelled utilization. Works for machines, trucks and any other asset.

Utilisation (utilization) rate is the share of the hours an asset was available that it actually worked. This page sets out the formula, a worked example with made-up numbers, and a calculator you fill in yourself.

The formula

  • Utilisation rate (%) = hours worked ÷ hours available × 100
  • Variance against target (percentage points) = utilisation rate minus your target rate
  • Hours needed to reach target = (target rate × hours available) minus hours worked
  • Owning cost per worked hour = owning cost for the period ÷ hours worked
  • Cost of unused hours = owning cost for the period × (1 − utilisation rate)

Hours available is whatever you decide an asset could work in the period, for example a standard working week. State your definition and use it every time. The rate is only comparable between assets, or between months, if hours available is defined the same way.

Why utilisation matters

An asset costs money to own whether it works or not, so hours that are not worked still carry that cost. The calculator's cost of unused hours shows that. You can also work out your machine's cost per hour.

Frequently asked questions

What is utilisation rate?

Utilisation (utilization) rate is the share of the hours an asset was available that it actually worked. It is hours worked divided by hours available, times 100.

How do you calculate fleet utilisation?

Divide hours worked by hours available, then multiply by 100. That is the fleet utilization formula. Use the same definition of hours available for every asset and every period you compare.

What counts as "hours available"?

Hours available is whatever you decide an asset could work in the period, for example a standard working week. State that definition and use it every time. The rate is only comparable between assets, or between months, if hours available is defined the same way.

Is utilisation the same as utilization?

Yes. Utilisation and utilization are the same measure with different spelling. This page uses the New Zealand spelling.
